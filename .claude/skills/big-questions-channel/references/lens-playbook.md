# Lens playbook: "measured against what?"

Most big questions have been covered by other channels. We stand out by angle, not by topic. Apply these steps to every episode before outlining.

## 1. Find the hidden reference
Every claim compares something with something. Ask:
- What is this measured against? (hot → the climate we evolved in; fast → the observer; smart → ourselves; "a day" → one turn of the Earth)
- Who or what is standing at zero?
- What would the answer be from another reference? Move the reference at least once in the video, on screen.

## 2. Add the engineer's test
- A back-of-envelope estimate with round numbers the viewer can follow.
- A real measurement or instrument (a thermometer, a clock, a ruler, a particle counter) and what it actually counts.
- "How would we test this?" Even a thought experiment counts if it is concrete.

## 3. Check the gap
Before scripting, look at what the most popular existing videos on the topic say. Write down in one line what they skip. If they already do what we planned, change the twist or park the topic.

## 4. Make every step drawable
Each reasoning step gets one concrete, everyday example that can be illustrated (a measuring tape, a campfire, a pear on a tree). If a step can't be drawn, it's too abstract: find the example first.

## 5. Connect episodes
Where it is honest to do so, link to an earlier or later episode (e.g. the body's temperature range in episode 2 returns in the fear-of-the-dark episode). Series and callbacks make viewers watch more than one video.

## Worked examples

| Plain topic | Our version | Reference that moves |
|---|---|---|
| Why does time feel faster as we age? | Time flies, measured against what? | A year as a share of your whole life |
| Were our ancestors smarter? | Smarter by whose measure? | The individual vs the society |
| Is anything measured absolutely? | Ten metres from whom? | The person holding the tape |
| Could humans live on Mars? | Habitable compared to what? | Earth's harshest places, not its average |
| How small is a chip? | How small, compared to what? | A hair, a virus, an atom |

## Things the lens is not
- Not relativism for its own sake: always land on what we can say once the reference is fixed.
- Not a gimmick line repeated in every title. Use the phrase "compared to what?" in titles sparingly; the lens shows in the reasoning.
